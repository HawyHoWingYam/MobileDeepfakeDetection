Deepfakes pose growing risks to trust, safety, and platform integrity. Practical countermeasures must operate robustly and efficiently on resource--constrained devices. We target a mobile deployment setting with the following challenges: (i) cross--dataset generalization in the wild, (ii) low latency and small footprint, and (iii) strict control of false negatives (missed fakes). Building on our early project plan (paper\_related) that explored lightweight CNNs, quantization, and possible knowledge distillation, we concretize a cascaded design that achieves favorable accuracy--efficiency trade-offs while preserving on-device privacy.

Recent detectors increasingly adopt Transformer or hybrid CNN\hyp ViT models (e.g., Swin, DeiT) that improve accuracy but raise compute and memory costs, complicating mobile deployment \cite{dosovitskiy2021vit,liu2021swin,touvron2021deit,khan2022hybrid_transformer_df}. At the same time, cross\hyp dataset evaluations consistently report notable performance drops from curated benchmarks to in\hyp the\hyp wild media \cite{wilddeepfake2021,pei2024deepfakesurvey,verdoliva2020forensics_survey}, reinforcing our focus on a cascade tuned for low false negatives under explicit efficiency budgets.

Concretely, MobileDeepfake follows a train\,\textrightarrow\,tune\,\textrightarrow\,deploy workflow across six stages specified in our project documents (project\_instruction). Stage~1 trains a lightweight MobileNetV4 filter on multiple academic datasets using balanced sampling. Stage~2 (and Stage~3 in our code) mines a difficult subset and trains an EfficientNetV2 expert to resolve ambiguous cases. Stage~4 then performs a grid search over low/high decision thresholds to minimize false negatives under explicit efficiency constraints, yielding a cascade that defers only a small fraction of samples to Stage~2. Stage~5 conducts cross\hyp dataset evaluation (e.g., Deepfake\hyp Eval\hyp 2024), and Stage~6 exports TorchScript artifacts for on\hyp device integration. Throughout, we log metrics and artifacts under \verb|outputs/| and auto\hyp generate paper tables/figures to ensure reproducibility.

We design MobileDeepfake, a two--stage cascade: a lightweight MobileNetV4 (Stage~1) rapidly filters confident cases; ambiguous samples are escalated to an EfficientNetV2 expert (Stage~2). A threshold grid search over low/high decision boundaries optimizes the cascade under accuracy and F1 constraints while minimizing false negatives and escalation rate. The full pipeline includes difficult\hyp sample mining, expert training on hard cases, comprehensive evaluation across datasets, and PyTorch Mobile deployment. For clarity, our contribution is not a new network block but a system design and evaluation methodology that prioritizes on\hyp device deployability while maintaining strong detection quality.

This paper builds on our midterm and early project reports (see paper\_related) and the stage plans/reports (project\_instruction). \subsection*{Open Challenges in Mobile Deepfake Detection}
Despite strong in\hyp distribution results reported by many detectors, three practical challenges persist: (i) cross\hyp dataset generalization under distribution shift—large performance drops are widely reported when moving from curated to in\hyp the\hyp wild media \cite{wilddeepfake2021,pei2024deepfakesurvey,verdoliva2020forensics_survey}; (ii) principled management of the accuracy\,–\,latency\,–\,memory trade\,off on mobile devices, beyond generic compression; and (iii) security\hyp oriented control of false negatives that conflicts with efficiency. Our system design and evaluation target these gaps by exposing tunable dials (thresholds, temperatures) aligned with operational budgets and by reporting escalation rates alongside accuracy metrics.
