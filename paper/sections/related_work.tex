\subsection*{Detection Architectures}

\subsubsection*{CNN-Based Approaches}
On early deepfake benchmarks such as FaceForensics++ and DFDC, Xception\hyp based CNNs became de facto baselines~\cite{rossler2019faceforensicspp,dolhansky2020dfdc,li2020celebdf}. EfficientNet~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} and its successor EfficientNetV2~\cite{efficientnetv2} achieved strong performance through compound scaling that optimizes depth, width, and resolution jointly. Recent studies~\cite{zhao2022mobilenet_lstm,liu2024lightfakedetect} demonstrate that even lightweight architectures like MobileNetV3~\cite{howard2019mobilenetv3} can achieve competitive detection accuracy when properly fine\hyp tuned. ResNet\hyp family models~\cite{he2016resnet}, including ResNeXt variants, have been widely adopted for spatial feature extraction in deepfake detection~\cite{chandra2024dfeval}. Informed by these CNN architectures and mobile efficiency considerations, we adopt MobileNetV4 for our Stage~1 filter and EfficientNetV2 for our Stage~2 expert, balancing detection accuracy with on\hyp device latency constraints.

\subsubsection*{Frequency-Domain Methods}
Frequency analysis has emerged as a powerful approach for detecting deepfake artifacts that survive spatial-domain compression. Durall et al.~\cite{durall2020watch} demonstrated that GAN-generated images exhibit distinctive frequency patterns in their spectral distributions. Qian et al.~\cite{qian2020thinkingfrequency} proposed adaptive frequency filtering to emphasize manipulation cues in the frequency domain. More recent work~\cite{luo2024freqnet,zhang2024frequency_dcT} leverages Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to extract high\hyp frequency artifacts as forensic fingerprints. FreqNet~\cite{luo2024freqnet}, introduced at AAAI 2024, applies convolutional layers to both phase and amplitude spectra between FFT and inverse FFT operations, achieving state\hyp of\hyp the\hyp art generalization (+9.8\% improvement) across 17 GANs. Studies using Discrete Fourier Transform~\cite{jablonski2024dft} report classification accuracies exceeding 99\% when frequency-domain features are combined with traditional ML classifiers. Wang et al.~\cite{wang2022afd} proposed Adaptive Frequency learning (AFD) that dynamically adjusts frequency emphasis during training to improve robustness under compression. While we focus on spatial\hyp domain CNN architectures for simplicity and inference speed, frequency\hyp domain extensions remain compatible with our cascade framework and could provide complementary robustness under post\hyp processing.

\subsubsection*{Vision Transformers and Hybrid Architectures}
Vision Transformers (ViTs) have recently been adapted to deepfake detection with promising results. Vanilla ViT~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} and hierarchical variants such as Swin Transformer~\cite{liu2021swin} provide global receptive fields that can capture subtle manipulation artifacts across the entire image. A comprehensive 2024 survey~\cite{khan2024vit_survey} categorizes 14 ViT\hyp based deepfake detection models into standalone, sequential (e.g., MARLIN, ISTVT), and parallel (e.g., M2TR) architectures. UIA\hyp ViT~\cite{zhang2022uiavit} achieved 99.33\% AUC on FaceForensics++ by incorporating uncertainty\hyp informed attention mechanisms.

Hybrid CNN\hyp Transformer architectures combine the inductive biases of CNNs with the global modeling capacity of transformers. Coccomini et al.~\cite{coccomini2022efficientnet_vit} combined EfficientNet and vision transformers for video deepfake detection in 2022, while Khan and Dang\hyp Nguyen~\cite{khan2022hybrid_transformer_df} proposed a Hybrid Transformer Network that fuses convolutional features with transformer encodings. GenConViT~\cite{genconvit_github}, which we explore in our Stage~2 expert ensemble, represents a generative convolutional vision transformer that operates in dual modes (hybrid and pretrained). CoAtNet\hyp based ensembles~\cite{naz2023coatnet} leverage self\hyp attention mechanisms in bagging frameworks for manipulated face detection. Das et al.~\cite{das2023mae_spatiotemporal} introduced masked autoencoding with spatiotemporal transformers for enhanced video forgery detection.

\subsubsection*{Attention Mechanisms and Multi-Scale Analysis}
Attention mechanisms have proven effective in focusing on manipulation artifacts. Multi\hyp attention networks~\cite{zhao2025multiattention} combining region\hyp independent loss and attention\hyp guided data augmentation achieved state\hyp of\hyp the\hyp art performance across multiple datasets. ReLAF\hyp Net~\cite{wang2024relafnet} employs restricted self\hyp attention on deep CNN features to learn both fine\hyp grained local relationships and global dependencies. MobileNetV3 augmented with self\hyp attention layers~\cite{khan2024mobilenet_attention} demonstrated that lightweight architectures can benefit significantly from attention mechanisms while maintaining deployment efficiency. Spatiotemporal attention mechanisms~\cite{zhang2024adlnet,xception_convlstm2022} have been integrated with ConvLSTM to capture and enhance frame\hyp to\hyp frame correlations in video sequences.

\subsubsection*{Temporal and Video-Level Detection}
Beyond spatial architectures, temporal modeling helps handle partial manipulations and contextual cues in video~\cite{wilddeepfake2021,undercover2023}. 3D CNNs~\cite{tran2015c3d} extend 2D convolutional filters to spatiotemporal dimensions, enabling direct learning from video clips. The I3D (Inflated 3D) model~\cite{carreira2017i3d} modifies 2D Inception networks by inflating convolutional and pooling kernels to 3D. Hybrid CNN\hyp LSTM architectures~\cite{zhao2022mobilenet_lstm,zhou2023resnext_lstm} use CNNs for spatial feature extraction at the frame level, then feed sequential features into LSTMs for temporal modeling. Xception\hyp LSTM with ConvLSTM~\cite{xception_convlstm2022} incorporates spatiotemporal attention before dimension reduction to preserve frame structure information. EfficientNet\hyp Bi\hyp LSTM~\cite{wang2023efficientnet_bilstm} and similar approaches extract spatial information via EfficientNet and acquire temporal information through bidirectional LSTMs. TimeSformer~\cite{bertasius2021timesformer} and other video transformers provide pure transformer\hyp based alternatives for spatiotemporal reasoning.

Parameter\hyp efficient adapters (e.g., DeepFake\hyp Adapter~\cite{liu2023deepfake_adapter}) further improve transfer to in\hyp the\hyp wild data by tuning small sets of adapter parameters on top of a frozen backbone, reducing computational cost while maintaining generalization. Other lines exploit physiological signals (e.g., eye blinking~\cite{yang2019exposing}, head motion) and camera fingerprints~\cite{cozzolino2019noiseprint}, but typically rely on specialized preprocessing pipelines. In this work we focus on frame\hyp level classification for reproducibility and mobile latency, using generic image backbones (MobileNetV4 and EfficientNetV2) and optional GenConViT experts. Our pipeline remains compatible with temporal post\hyp processing when budgets allow, but deliberately keeps the core inference path simple enough for on\hyp device deployment.

\subsection*{Lightweight Models and Compression}

\subsubsection*{Efficient Architecture Design}
Mobile vision emphasizes efficient backbones tailored to on\hyp device constraints. The MobileNet family~\cite{howard2017mobilenetv1,sandler2018mobilenetv2,howard2019mobilenetv3} pioneered depthwise separable convolutions to reduce computational cost while maintaining accuracy. MobileNetV3~\cite{howard2019mobilenetv3} incorporated hardware\hyp aware NAS and squeeze\hyp and\hyp excitation modules to optimize for mobile CPUs and GPUs. The latest MobileNetV4~\cite{mobilenetv4} further scales up mobile CNNs with hybrid architectures and improved training recipes. EfficientNet~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} and its successor EfficientNetV2~\cite{efficientnetv2} deliver strong accuracy under tight FLOPs budgets through compound scaling that jointly optimizes network depth, width, and resolution. ShuffleNet~\cite{zhang2018shufflenet,ma2018shufflenetv2} and SqueezeNet~\cite{iandola2016squeezenet} provide alternative efficient designs that prioritize inference speed on resource\hyp constrained devices.

Hardware\hyp aware neural architecture search (NAS)~\cite{tan2019mnasnet} and once\hyp for\hyp all training~\cite{cai2020onceforall} enable per\hyp device specialization from a single supernet, allowing practitioners to extract task\hyp and\hyp device\hyp specific sub\hyp networks without retraining from scratch. Recent real\hyp time deepfake detection systems~\cite{lanzino2024bnn,liu2024lightfakedetect} demonstrate that lightweight models such as MobileNetV3 and binary neural networks can achieve competitive performance when combined with attention mechanisms and proper fine\hyp tuning strategies.

\subsubsection*{Model Compression Techniques}
Pruning and sparsity methods reduce model size by removing redundant weights and connections. Structured pruning removes entire channels or filters, while unstructured pruning targets individual weights~\cite{blalock2020pruningsurvey}. Han et al.~\cite{han2015learningboth,han2016deepcompression} demonstrated that deep networks can be compressed by 10-50× through iterative pruning, quantization, and Huffman coding. The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis~\cite{frankle2019lottery} suggests that dense networks contain sparse sub\hyp networks that can achieve comparable accuracy when trained from the right initialization. Recent surveys~\cite{blalock2020pruningsurvey} provide comprehensive comparisons of pruning methods and their impact on inference efficiency.

Knowledge distillation~\cite{hinton2015distilling} transfers knowledge from a large teacher model to a compact student model by matching soft probability distributions or intermediate feature representations. FitNets~\cite{romero2015fitnets} extend this idea by matching hint layers at intermediate depths, while Born\hyp Again Networks~\cite{furlanello2018bornagain} apply multi\hyp generation distillation where students can outperform teachers. Recent surveys~\cite{gou2021kdsurvey} synthesize these trends and highlight that distillation is particularly effective when combined with pruning and quantization. Studies on knowledge distillation for edge deployment~\cite{zhao2023kd_edge,liu2024medical_kd} demonstrate that distilled models can achieve 70\% compression with minimal accuracy degradation, making them suitable for fog/edge devices in applications like automatic disease diagnosis and on\hyp device deepfake detection.

\subsubsection*{Quantization for Mobile Deployment}
Quantization has become a central lever for efficient inference, reducing both model size and computational cost by representing weights and activations with lower\hyp precision data types. Post\hyp training quantization (PTQ)~\cite{jacob2017integeronly,esser2020lsq} converts trained FP32 models to INT8 or lower bitwidths without retraining, offering a fast and lightweight approach to compression. Quantization\hyp aware training (QAT)~\cite{esser2020lsq,wang2019haq} simulates quantization during training, enabling lower bitwidths (e.g., INT4) with competitive accuracy but requiring access to training data and greater computational expense. A 2021 white paper~\cite{gholami2021quant_survey} provides a comprehensive survey of quantization methods, highlighting that PTQ is sufficient for 8\hyp bit quantization with near\hyp FP32 accuracy, while QAT enables aggressive sub\hyp 8\hyp bit compression.

Mixed\hyp precision and per\hyp channel quantization schemes~\cite{wang2019haq,esser2020lsq} provide competitive accuracy at low bitwidths by allowing different layers or channels to use different quantization scales. Recent work on quantizing large language models~\cite{frantar2023gptq,yao2022zeroquant} and vision transformers demonstrates that quantization remains effective even for modern architectures. For deepfake detection, quantization enables deployment on commodity mobile devices with minimal accuracy loss, and modern deep learning frameworks provide end\hyp to\hyp end toolchains for converting research models into compact INT8 variants and exporting them to mobile platforms.

Our Stage~1 uses MobileNetV4 as a fast filter, and Stage~2 adopts EfficientNetV2 and optional GenConViT experts~\cite{efficientnetv2,genconvit_github}. Stage~4 builds on QAT + knowledge distillation in a teacher–student setup and compares FP32 vs.\ INT8 models in terms of accuracy, size, and latency, while Stage~6 exports TorchScript and ONNX artifacts suitable for mobile integration. We emphasize measurable footprint and latency on device: model size, on\hyp device escalation rate, and duty cycle jointly determine energy and user experience, so we report escalation alongside accuracy when comparing options.

\subsection*{Cascade and Adaptive Inference}

\subsubsection*{Early-Exit Networks and Dynamic Depth}
Early\hyp exit and cascaded systems trade accuracy for efficiency by routing easy samples to fast exits and escalating hard ones. BranchyNet~\cite{teerapittayanon2017branchynet} pioneered this approach by attaching shallow classifiers to intermediate layers of deep networks, enabling early termination for confident predictions. Multi\hyp Scale Dense Networks (MSDNet)~\cite{huang2018msdnet} extend this idea by learning feature representations at multiple scales, allowing classifiers at different depths to leverage complementary information. Shallow\hyp Deep Networks~\cite{kaya2019shallowdeep} address network overthinking by identifying when deeper layers degrade performance on easy samples.

A comprehensive 2024 survey on early\hyp exit deep neural networks~\cite{han2024earlyexit_survey} categorizes methods into static (BranchyNet, MSDNet) and adaptive (ACT, SkipNet) approaches. Adaptive Computation Time (ACT)~\cite{graves2016act} for RNNs and SkipNet~\cite{wang2018skipnet} for CNNs learn dynamic routing policies that adapt network depth to input complexity. Recent work on window\hyp based early\hyp exit cascades~\cite{xia2023window_cascade} incorporates uncertainty estimation for improved reliability. QuickNets~\cite{han2022quicknets} introduced a novel cascaded training algorithm where each successive layer is trained only on samples misclassified by previous layers, preventing overconfidence and improving calibration.

\subsubsection*{Routing Policies and Threshold Selection}
Confidence\hyp, entropy\hyp, and margin\hyp based routing are common strategies for determining when to exit early. Hendrycks and Gimpel~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline} established maximum softmax probability as a simple baseline for detecting misclassified examples and out\hyp of\hyp distribution samples. Cost\hyp sensitive learning frameworks~\cite{elkan2001cost} formalize the trade\hyp off between classification errors and computational costs, enabling threshold selection that minimizes expected cost rather than maximizing accuracy alone. Recent work increasingly frames threshold selection as a multi\hyp objective optimization problem that balances error rates (false positives, false negatives) against compute budgets (FLOPs, latency, energy)~\cite{laskaridis2020hapi,laskaridis2020spinn}.

Cascaded systems for object detection~\cite{viola2001rapid} and classification have been deployed successfully in production environments, but typically employ hand\hyp tuned thresholds rather than learned policies. Bayesian optimization~\cite{snoek2012bayesopt} and grid search remain popular for threshold tuning when the search space is small. For multi\hyp stage systems, joint optimization of all thresholds is often intractable, motivating greedy stage\hyp wise tuning or heuristic policies.

\subsubsection*{Cascades in Deepfake Detection}
In deepfake detection, cascades are less explored than single\hyp model detectors, and are rarely paired with explicit system\hyp level metrics such as Stage~1 leakage rate and Stage~2 escalation rate. Most prior work reports AUC/F1 on a fixed test set without exposing the operational trade\hyp offs platform operators care about. Classical cascade detectors like Viola\hyp Jones~\cite{viola2001rapid} for face detection demonstrated the effectiveness of multi\hyp stage architectures, but deepfake detection has largely focused on maximizing single\hyp model accuracy rather than optimizing inference efficiency.

Our system follows the early\hyp exit lineage but makes the design explicitly cost\hyp aware: a lightweight Stage~1 filter (MobileNetV4) sits in front of a stronger Stage~2 expert (EfficientNetV2 + GenConViT) and optional Stage~3 meta\hyp model (LightGBM), and a two\hyp threshold routing scheme defines a tunable ``uncertainty band'' for escalation. Stage~4 performs grid search over low/high thresholds using cached logits, optimizing for low false negative rate under an escalation\hyp rate budget (typically 1–5\%). The resulting operating points are reported with both FNR and Stage~2 escalation rate, exposing interpretable trade\hyp offs between risk and compute. For deployment, we keep the runtime policy intentionally simple: thresholds are tuned offline via grid search and then fixed on device for stability, avoiding the complexity of learned routing policies that may drift under distribution shift.

\subsection*{Generalization and Robustness}

\subsubsection*{Cross-Dataset Generalization}
Cross\hyp dataset generalization is a core challenge in deepfake detection: large AUC drops (often 20–30\%) are observed when models trained on curated datasets like FaceForensics++ are evaluated on in\hyp the\hyp wild media~\cite{wilddeepfake2021,pei2024deepfakesurvey}. State\hyp of\hyp the\hyp art models achieve 96–99\% AUC on in\hyp dataset tests but degrade to 72–78\% on cross\hyp dataset evaluations~\cite{chandra2024dfeval,zhang2024deepfakebench}. This performance gap stems from dataset bias, compression artifacts, and differences in manipulation techniques across generators.

Domain Adaptive Batch Normalization (DABN)~\cite{li2021dabn} mitigates distribution gaps by utilizing test\hyp set statistics to restore batch normalization layer effectiveness, achieving nearly 20\% accuracy improvement on CelebDF under black\hyp box settings. Recent work on unsupervised domain adaptation~\cite{liu2025unsupervised_da} transfers deepfake detection knowledge from labeled source domains to unlabeled target domains using Domain Distance Optimization (DDO) modules that align inter\hyp domain and intra\hyp domain feature distances. Spatial\hyp frequency collaborative learning methods~\cite{wang2025spatial_freq} outperform frequency\hyp aware baselines by 9.5\% AUC on CelebDF through joint spatial and frequency domain modeling.

Domain adaptation surveys~\cite{wang2018deepDA_survey,ganin2016dann} provide comprehensive taxonomies of transfer learning and domain\hyp adversarial approaches. Domain\hyp Adversarial Neural Networks (DANN)~\cite{ganin2016dann} learn domain\hyp invariant features through adversarial training between feature extractors and domain classifiers. However, achieving consistent cross\hyp dataset performance remains elusive: fairness\hyp aware methods~\cite{zhang2024fairness_generalization} maintain fairness metrics on some test sets but fail on others, highlighting the difficulty of generalizing both accuracy and fairness simultaneously. In our work, we train on a combined multi\hyp dataset manifest and evaluate on Deepfake\hyp Eval\hyp 2024 as an explicit OOD benchmark to directly measure this generalization gap and report cross\hyp dataset performance honestly.

\subsubsection*{Robustness to Perturbations and Corruptions}
Robustness to common corruptions (JPEG compression, noise, blur, illumination changes) is likewise uneven; even strong detectors can degrade substantially under realistic post\hyp processing~\cite{durall2020watch,qian2020thinkingfrequency,wang2022afd}. Frequency\hyp domain methods provide one avenue for robustness by emphasizing manipulation cues that survive compression and noise. Adaptive frequency learning~\cite{wang2022afd} dynamically adjusts frequency emphasis during training to improve robustness under JPEG compression. High\hyp frequency enhancement networks~\cite{zhao2024hfen} for heavily compressed content demonstrate that explicit frequency modeling can recover detection performance even under aggressive compression.

Adversarial robustness is another critical dimension. Carlini and Wagner~\cite{carlini2017towards} attacks and Projected Gradient Descent (PGD)~\cite{madry2018towards} perturbations can fool deepfake detectors with imperceptible modifications. While adversarial training~\cite{madry2018towards,goodfellow2015explaining} improves robustness to $\ell_p$-bounded attacks, it often degrades performance on clean samples and does not guarantee robustness to adaptive attacks. Recent comprehensive evaluations~\cite{gandhi2024comprehensive_eval} assess 8 state\hyp of\hyp the\hyp art defenses against evolving threats (adversarial examples, OOD samples, novel generators), finding that no single method achieves robustness across all threat models.

\subsubsection*{Benchmarks and Evaluation Protocols}
Newer benchmarks explicitly target generalization gaps. Deepfake\hyp Eval\hyp 2024~\cite{deepeval2024} aggregates 1,072 real and 964 fake files from diverse platforms, languages, and compression pipelines as an in\hyp the\hyp wild benchmark. DeepfakeBench~\cite{zhang2024deepfakebench}, presented at NeurIPS 2023, provides a comprehensive evaluation framework supporting 36 detection methods (28 image + 8 video detectors) and introduces the DF40 dataset comprising 40 distinct deepfake techniques including state\hyp of\hyp the\hyp art 2024 generators. Celeb\hyp DF++~\cite{li2025celebdfpp} extends the original CelebDF benchmark to evaluate 19 classic methods and 5 recent (post\hyp 2024) detectors.

Recent surveys~\cite{pei2024deepfakesurvey,electronics2024survey,springer2025multimodal} emphasize that robustness techniques must be coupled with rigorous cross\hyp dataset evaluation protocols to avoid overfitting to specific artifacts. The surveys categorize methods by feature extraction (spatial, frequency, temporal, multimodal) and network architecture (CNN, Transformer, hybrid), and identify generalization as the primary open challenge for practical deployment.

\subsubsection*{Calibration and Confidence Estimation}
Calibration and thresholding play an important role under distribution shift. Probability calibration techniques such as temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration,minderer2021revisiting} help align predicted probabilities with empirical frequencies, improving reliability diagrams and expected calibration error (ECE). However, calibration does not eliminate performance gaps on new datasets~\cite{pei2024deepfakesurvey}; a well\hyp calibrated model with 75\% accuracy remains poorly calibrated if evaluated on a distribution where its true accuracy is 60\%.

Ensemble and meta\hyp learning approaches~\cite{naskar2024ensemble,zhou2024deepfakestack} leverage stacking\hyp based fusion to improve both accuracy and calibration. Naskar et al.~\cite{naskar2024ensemble} combined Xception and EfficientNetV2-B7 features via a meta\hyp learner (MLP), achieving 93\% accuracy on CelebDF and 98\% on FaceForensics++ through feature ranking and selective fusion. Our Stage~3 meta\hyp model follows this stacking paradigm but uses LightGBM as the meta\hyp learner and incorporates K\hyp fold cross\hyp validation for robustness.

In our evaluation (Stage~5), we therefore combine cross\hyp dataset experiments on Deepfake\hyp Eval\hyp 2024 with robustness sweeps over JPEG, noise, motion blur, brightness, downscaling, and gamma perturbations. We report both calibrated cascade results and a Stage~2\hyp only upper bound, and we log Stage~2 escalation rates alongside F1/FNR. This design emphasizes system\hyp level trade\hyp offs: robustness gains are only useful if they do not explode compute or latency on device, and calibration is only useful when interpreted jointly with escalation and leakage statistics rather than as a standalone score.

\subsection*{Deployment and Tooling}
Production deployment for mobile requires artifact export, footprint accounting, latency measurement, and minimal telemetry. TorchScript- or ONNX\hyp style runtimes provide stable toolchains for shipping models to Android and iOS, while quantization tooling and surveys~\cite{gholami2021quant_survey} make it feasible to convert research models into compact INT8 variants without rewriting training code. Beyond the model itself, practitioners need configuration management (e.g., thresholds, calibration temperatures), logging and monitoring hooks, and a clear separation between offline tuning and online inference.

Our pipeline integrates these considerations end\hyp to\hyp end. We export TorchScript artifacts for Stage~1 and Stage~2 and a bundle metadata file that records thresholds, calibration temperatures, and version information. ONNX exporters and benchmarking scripts provide optional support for alternative runtimes and detailed latency/memory measurements. We also minimise I/O and allocations on device to reduce jitter and simplify maintenance. This positions \textbf{MobileDeepfake} as a bridge between research prototypes and production systems: we emphasise (i) multi\hyp dataset training and hard\hyp example mining; (ii) cost\hyp sensitive threshold tuning for low false negatives with controlled escalation; and (iii) on\hyp device integration with measurable trade\hyp offs. Unlike single\hyp model detectors, our system exposes designer\hyp visible dials (thresholds, temperature) aligned with operational requirements and supported by reproducible scripts and artifacts.
