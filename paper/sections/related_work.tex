Deepfake detection research spans spatial, frequency, and multimodal cues, as well as dataset curation and evaluation protocols~\cite{verdoliva2020forensics_survey,pei2024deepfakesurvey}. Approaches range from handcrafted forensic features (e.g., head pose, blink patterns, compression artifacts) to modern CNN/Transformer models. Recent in\hyp the\hyp wild datasets emphasize distribution shift and multilingual, multi\hyp source coverage, motivating cross\hyp dataset generalization as a first\hyp class objective.

\subsection*{Detection Architectures}
CNN baselines (e.g., Xception, ResNet variants) remain strong on in\hyp distribution benchmarks; Transformer and hybrid CNN\hyp ViT models push accuracy further but at higher cost \cite{chollet2017xception,dosovitskiy2021vit,khan2022hybrid_transformer_df,das2023mae_spatiotemporal}. On FaceForensics++ and DFDC, Xception\-like models are widely used strong baselines \cite{rossler2019faceforensicspp,dolhansky2020dfdc}. Beyond spatial CNNs, compact detectors (e.g., MesoNet) and forensic approaches (MANTra\-Net, Noiseprint) provide low\-overhead or interpretable signals \cite{afchar2018mesonet,wu2019mantra,cozzolino2019noiseprint}. Temporal modeling (3D CNNs/RNNs) helps handle partial manipulations \cite{wilddeepfake2021}; generic video encoders such as C3D demonstrate the utility of 3D convolutions for spatiotemporal features \cite{tran2015c3d}. Hierarchical ViTs (e.g., Swin) and data\hyp efficient ViT training with distillation (DeiT) further illustrate accuracy/efficiency trade\hyp offs relevant to mobile constraints \cite{liu2021swin,touvron2021deit}. Parameter\-efficient ViT adaptation (e.g., DeepFake\-Adapter) has shown improved cross\-dataset generalization \cite{liu2023deepfake_adapter}.

\subsection*{Lightweight Models and Compression}
Mobile vision emphasizes efficient backbones (MobileNet families, ShuffleNet, EfficientNet\hyp Lite) and compression (quantization, pruning, distillation) \cite{mobilenetv4,howard2017mobilenetv1,sandler2018mobilenetv2,howard2019mobilenetv3,zhang2018shufflenet,ma2018shufflenetv2,blalock2020pruningsurvey,gou2021kdsurvey}. Classical small\hyp footprint designs (e.g., SqueezeNet) and platform\hyp aware NAS (e.g., MnasNet) push efficiency on devices \cite{iandola2016squeezenet,tan2019mnasnet}; once\hyp for\hyp all training enables per\hyp device specialization from a single supernet \cite{cai2020onceforall}. These techniques reduce compute and memory while preserving accuracy. Our Stage~1 uses MobileNetV4; Stage~2 adopts EfficientNetV2~\cite{efficientnetv2,tan2019efficientnet}. We rely on TorchScript for export and PyTorch Mobile runtime~\cite{torchscript,pytorch_mobile}; \texttt{torchao} quantization offers deeper per\hyp channel/mixed precision options \cite{torchao}.

\subsection*{Cascade and Adaptive Inference}
Early\hyp exit and cascaded systems trade accuracy for efficiency by routing easy samples to fast exits and escalating hard ones \cite{teerapittayanon2017branchynet,kaya2019shallowdeep,huang2018msdnet,wang2018skipnet,graves2016act}. Confidence\hyp, entropy\hyp, and margin\hyp based routing are common; threshold selection may be cost\hyp sensitive under budgets \cite{elkan2001cost}. Historically, cascade ideas trace back to Viola\,–\,Jones face detection \cite{viola2001rapid}. We follow this lineage by exposing a practical knob on the escalation rate, calibrating thresholds under a budget, and keeping the mobile implementation simple and stable.

\subsection*{Generalization and Robustness}
Cross\hyp dataset generalization is a core challenge—large AUC drops are often observed from curated to in\hyp the\hyp wild data \cite{wilddeepfake2021}. Robustness to compression, noise, blur, and illumination changes also degrades performance \cite{pei2024deepfakesurvey,durall2020watch,qian2020thinkingfrequency,li2020facexray}. Two\-branch frequency methods and adaptive frequency learning (AFD) provide data/\-task adaptive frequency fusion \cite{wang2022afd}. In addition to natural perturbations, adversarial perturbations can severely impact deep detectors \cite{goodfellow2015explaining,madry2018towards}; reporting calibration and thresholding alongside accuracy is therefore important. We reflect this insight in OOD evaluation (Deepfake\hyp Eval\hyp 2024) and in per\-family threshold/temperature calibration under controlled perturbations.

\subsection*{Deployment and Tooling}
Production deployment for mobile requires artifact export, footprint accounting, latency measurement, and minimal telemetry. TorchScript and PyTorch Mobile provide a stable toolchain; quantization (PTQ/QAT) and distillation remain the primary levers \cite{jacob2017integeronly,gou2021kdsurvey}. Our pipeline integrates these considerations end\hyp to\hyp end.

We position our cascade by emphasizing: (i) multi\hyp dataset training and hard\hyp example mining; (ii) cost\hyp sensitive threshold tuning for low false negatives with controlled escalation; and (iii) on\hyp device integration with measurable trade\hyp offs. Unlike single\hyp model detectors, our system exposes designer\hyp visible dials (thresholds, temperature) aligned with operational requirements.
